# AI expectations and public services

A descriptive analysis of public attitudes to AI by Maham Sufi, using CDEI / DSIT Wave 3 survey data.

## Findings from the downloaded data

The weighted positive share is 52.4% for everyday tasks and 50.8% for healthcare. For fair treatment in society, 22.1% expect a positive impact and 31.1% a negative one. For job opportunities, the negative share is 43.3%.

For crime prevention and detection, 44.5% expect a positive impact, 18.7% a negative impact, 29.3% are neutral and 7.5% select Don't know. Positive expectations are 48.7% among ages 18–34, 43.4% among ages 35–54 and 42.4% among ages 55+. These are descriptive differences, not tests of significance. 66.4% report being able to explain AI at least partially; that is self-assessment, not a skills test.

## Interpretation for qualitative research

The pattern motivates asking participants about particular uses of AI rather than treating AI as one uniform intervention. Possible follow-up questions: What makes a use helpful? What could make it unfair? Who should explain or challenge a decision? These are suggested research questions, not findings about justice service users.

## Method and limits

Data are from the CDEI / DSIT Wave 3 online survey, fielded 11–23 August 2023. The 4,225 rows represent respondents. We use the supplied weights and divide each category's weight sum by the weight sum for all respondents in its group. Positive combines Very and Slightly positive; Negative combines Very and Slightly negative. Don't know is retained. Blank answers would remain a separate Missing category; none occur in the selected answers in this release. No responses were imputed or removed.

These are historical 2023 perceptions, not current views or measured effects of AI. The separate telephone sample is not included. The study uses an online quota sample; weighting does not remove all coverage or selection bias. No confidence intervals or population significance claims are made here. General expectations about crime do not establish approval of any specific AI tool, fairness, or outcomes for people using justice services.
